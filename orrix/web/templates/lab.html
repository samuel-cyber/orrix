Attack Lab

Paste a hostile buyer message, or pick an author-written preset, and run the same text through two systems at once. A dry run: fake tools only, the configured model is the only thing that reaches the wire, and no write to PayPal is reachable from here.

{# Honest labelling, stated plainly, before any result. #}

This is a comparison, not a demonstration. The left lane is a deliberately naive agent: it receives the entire dispute record — buyer message included, with nothing separating it from its instructions — in one turn, and holds a dry-run accept_dispute_claim tool. When it reaches for that tool, a red UNAUTHORIZED marker records a real action it took, not a rehearsal of one.

The right lane is Orrix: the reader turns the buyer text into a validated claim form, policy decides in plain code, and the most that can come out is an approval id — exactly what the console's approve button would mint, minus the write. Outcomes vary by model and are shown as they happen; no result is fabricated. Runs go to the lab's own table, never the hash-chained audit log.
